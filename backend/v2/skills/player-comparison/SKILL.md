# Player comparison

## Define what better means

Resolve whether the decision is about current play, fit, asset value, or a future horizon. Pin both players to comparable seasons, roles, minutes, usage, team quality, and availability. Block asset-value conclusions when the contract vintage is missing.

## Build competing cases

State the best case for each player. List the evidence that would separate the cases. Compare independent lenses, not the number of returned rows.

Use these lenses:

- role and possession burden;
- scoring volume and efficiency;
- creation, passing, and decision quality;
- shooting, spacing, and off-ball value;
- defense by assignment;
- rebounding and positional size;
- availability and sample stability;
- adjusted impact and contextualized on/off;
- role elasticity and playoff portability;
- age, health, contract, and scarcity when the decision is about asset value.

For current external context, use `web_search` -> `web_fetch`. Search snippets are discovery only. Keep measured performance separate from reported explanation. Never promote a search snippet into substantive evidence.

Keep metrics in lane. An impact estimate measures contribution. On/off diagnoses team changes and does not establish causality alone. Profile statistics explain mechanisms.

## Test contradictions

Explain apples-to-oranges roles, metric disagreement, shooting variance, teammate effects, and role changes. State the strongest counterargument to the likely winner.

## Complete the answer

Give conditional winners for the decision and horizon. Name where each player is better, which skills transfer, what depends on context, and what evidence cannot settle. End with the evidence or role change that would reverse the conclusion.
