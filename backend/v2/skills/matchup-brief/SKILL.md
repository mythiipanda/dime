# Matchup Brief

## When to use

Questions naming two teams for a pre-game brief, preview, or prediction.

## Key insights

- A brief compares two teams, never evaluates one team alone.
- Three narrative cards carry the story: best-on-best, secondary scoring, and x-factor.
- Label each card by claim kind: best-on-best is observed, secondary scoring is derived, x-factor is projection or judgment.
- Observed states what happened. Derived states what adjusted numbers say. Projection states what is likely next. Judgment states an opinion.

## What to fetch

- Ratings for both teams with pace alongside scoring efficiency.
- Splits for home, road, and recent form for both teams.
- Injuries for both teams plus the expected on-court impact of each absence.
- Season series results between the two teams.
- The modeled prediction with its confidence and limiting evidence.

## Caveats to apply

- Lineup evidence needs a possession floor and splits need a games floor. State each floor in every output.
- Small samples are noisy. Pair every small-sample number with its sample size.
- Quote pace alongside scoring efficiency in every comparison.
- Treat rest as a modifier to effort and late-game legs, never the lead reason for a pick.
- Tie every number to its evidence. Label projections and judgments as such.

## What NOT to do

- Never quote scoring efficiency without pace.
- Never lead with rest or make rest the prediction.
- Never present a number without its source and sample.
- Never present a projection or judgment as an observed fact.
